**Difference between an exon and an intron:**  
Exons are the regions of the gene structure shown as boxes or blocks. Introns are the regions located between the exons and are represented by the connecting lines. Exons and introns therefore have different positions and appearances within the gene structure.

**Introns compared with exons:**  
The introns in the TP53 gene generally appear longer than the exons. The exon boxes are relatively short, while the connecting regions between them are usually longer.

## Short Reflection

**1. What did UCSC show you about your gene that was not obvious from simply reading about the gene's function?**  
UCSC showed me the actual location and structure of the TP53 gene in the human genome. I was able to see its exons, introns, transcripts, and different annotation tracks.

**2. Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?**  
Knowing the exact genomic location helps identify where the variant is located in relation to the gene. It allows us to compare the variant with the gene structure and other genomic annotations.

**3. What is one limitation of predicting a variant's effect only from its genomic location?**  
A variant's location alone does not tell us exactly how it affects the gene or gene product. Additional evidence is needed to determine whether the variant has a functional effect or causes disease.

**4. What was the most interesting feature you observed about your assigned gene?**  
The most interesting feature I observed was the different transcripts and the exon-intron structure of TP53. I also found it interesting to see the ClinVar and conservation tracks alongside the gene.
